About

I recently finished my Ph.D. in Computer Science at UNIST (Aug. 2026), where I worked in the System Software Laboratory under Young-ri Choi.

My research designs the indexing and caching machinery inside key-value stores. I am interested in how these systems can stay efficient by adapting to the shape of the data and the pattern of the workload, rather than treating them as adversarial black boxes. Two projects give the flavor:

DyTIS (EuroSys 2023) is a dynamic hash index. Ordinary extendible hashing degrades when keys are skewed or the dataset drifts over time; DyTIS restructures itself in place so lookups stay near-constant and inserts remain cheap on the messy datasets that appear in practice.

TreePass (FAST 2027) is a B+-tree-aware buffer cache for SplinterDB. It tucks small counters into the tree's pivot entries; the cache reads them to spot cold subtrees and evict cold pages early, cutting write amplification without any extra bookkeeping.

I care about building things that are simple enough to reason about and small enough to land in a real system.
