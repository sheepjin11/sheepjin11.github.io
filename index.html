About

I recently finished my Ph.D. in Computer Science at UNIST (Aug. 2026), where I worked in the System Software Laboratory under Young-ri Choi. My research is on the indexing and caching machinery inside key-value stores — designs that adapt to how the data is shaped and how workloads actually access it, rather than treating those as adversarial black boxes.

Selected projects

TreePass — FAST 2027

A B+-tree-aware buffer cache for SplinterDB. TreePass tucks small counters into the tree's pivot entries; the cache reads them to spot cold subtrees and evict cold pages early, cutting write amplification without any extra bookkeeping.

DyTIS — EuroSys 2023

A dynamic hash index. Ordinary extendible hashing degrades when keys are skewed or the dataset drifts over time; DyTIS restructures itself in place so lookups stay near-constant and inserts remain cheap on the messy datasets that appear in practice.
